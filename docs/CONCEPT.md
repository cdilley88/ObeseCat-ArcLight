# Direction and world

## User direction

- Preserve the heavy atmosphere, deliberate pacing, graphics era, and style associated with Diablo 1 while adding modern quality of life and depth.
- Avoid generic medieval, demon, and high-fantasy settings. Grim Dawn is a directional reference, not a template to copy.
- Explore cats / ObeseCat, nuclear and Cold War themes, ITS colors, and CRT / 1990s–2000s glitch aesthetics.
- Recast recurring pet-inspired mascots as possible playable classes, with branching skill trees.
- Build toward acts and act bosses. ObeseCat could fill the role of the named final antagonist.
- After the visual identity, itemization is the primary gameplay priority.
- Progress into **glitched corruption**. Act I physically climbs UP from garage refuge to a corporate CEO office; the earlier descent is thematic, not a literal downward route.
- Alternate Cold War styling takes its cue from the user's 1990s Westwood Red Alert reference: underlying comical elements with brutally serious material. Oppressive Soviet styling is not the intended setting identity.
- Playable characters are four-legged pets. Equipped gear must be visible on their models and change their appearance; this is a high-priority immersion requirement.

## Proposal: how corruption escalates

Begin with a tangible alternate Cold War world of military experiments, nuclear hardware, command centers, and eccentric technology. As the characters progress, corruption increasingly affects physical reality: machinery repeats impossible motions, rooms acquire inconsistent geometry, and familiar creatures develop displaced or duplicated features. These examples are proposals; the origin and rules of corruption remain open.

Let the descent measure increasing corruption, whether travel goes underground, across regions, or through altered spaces. A single underground facility is not a settled campaign structure. The comedy can come from extravagant inventions and dead-serious institutional responses; danger and consequences remain credible.

## Earlier setting proposal: a facility that still thinks it is protecting you

This remains an optional location or premise, subordinate to the accepted alternate Cold War / glitched corruption direction.

A company town survives above a sealed civil-defense and nuclear research complex. Its systems are still running decades after evacuation. They continue issuing instructions, fabricating equipment, and enforcing a containment mission whose meaning has changed. The surviving pet characters descend to discover what is consuming the facility's power and changing the world above.

ObeseCat is the living center of this disaster. Its hunger could be literal, energetic, or something the facility mistakenly interprets as a maintenance problem. Keep its exact origin and moral role open: victim, failed guardian, or deliberate threat produce different endings.

This premise should generate gameplay: blast doors divide encounters; substations change routes; obsolete medical and industrial gear become loot; public-address warnings hint at threats. Nuclear language must shape mechanics and spaces, not just rename magic damage.

## Tone and presentation proposals

Express the accepted tonal mix through extravagant military science, characterful animal behavior, and institutions treating absurd situations with complete seriousness. Familiar pet silhouettes can make the danger more personal. Avoid deciding that every mascot belongs to the same species; confirm the roster from the user and supplied references.

Use an isometric camera, chunky low-resolution silhouettes, strong directional lighting, deep shadow, worn concrete, enamel, oxidized metal, and cramped machinery. Consider rendered sprites or low-poly models presented at a controlled resolution; production technique remains open.

Reserve cyan, magenta, and phosphor green for terminals, equipment states, rare phenomena, and selected loot cues. The environment needs enough quiet darkness for those colors to matter. Exact ITS palette values require supplied or verified references.

CRT distortion should concentrate in interfaces and anomalous events. Include readable text, reduced effects, and toggles for flicker, scanlines, and chromatic separation. Combat telegraphs and item comparisons must remain legible.

## Combat and quality-of-life proposals

Small dangerous groups, audible threats beyond sight, meaningful positioning, and time to examine a drop. Progression adds tactical options before it adds screen-clearing density. Avoid committing to dodge rolls or cooldown rotations until a combat slice proves they fit the intended pace.

Modern conveniences: responsive movement, rebindable inputs, clear comparisons, searchable shared storage, a customizable loot filter, automatic material pickup, transparent stat explanations, and a practical way to revise builds. Preserve tension in encounters rather than repetitive inventory chores.

## Character presentation: accepted direction

The user selected four-legged animals and requires visible equipment changes on the character models. Preserve recognizable bodies and pet silhouettes as their gear develops.

Proposed execution: fitted harnesses, collars, carried modules, and other equipment attached plausibly to the body. Equipment silhouette, placement, materials, and animation should communicate what is worn. Costs include unusual attack animation and fitting equipment across different pet bodies. The current slot direction is documented in ITEMIZATION; orthographic 3D is the approved prototype rendering approach. Visible equipment still guides the future art pipeline.

Do not assign permanent classes to named pets before confirming the roster, personalities, and visual references. Potential class roles include an armored close-range protector, a mobile ambusher, and a remote-device specialist. Each needs a distinctive play pattern rather than only a damage type.

Skill trees could offer three specializations per character, with cross-branch investment and gear-supported hybrids. Exact branch count, prerequisites, point economy, and respec cost are open.
